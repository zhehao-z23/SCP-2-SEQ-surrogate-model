# Why these measures answer different questions

Overall distribution Hellinger, the existing finite-cut CDF MSE, and task-level false negatives/false positives or recall at a fixed review budget answer different questions. They are retained separately; correlated shape descriptors are not combined into another leaderboard score.

The training objective is Hellinger squared, whereas checkpoint selection uses group-macro Hellinger after row-to-teacher-sequence-to-homology-group reduction. Online training loss also has dropout and changing parameters. It is not a fixed-state generalization gap. Comparing two saved models on train/stop/evaluation with dropout off supplies a limited common-state comparison, not a missing epoch trajectory.

At fixed cuts, the expected binary threshold Brier score minus a term depending only on the true distribution is the weighted squared difference of cumulative probabilities. This project-level derivation explains the existing equal-cut CDF MSE as a complementary ordered-bin diagnostic. It is not continuous raw-potential CRPS or W1, and it cannot identify within-bin shapes. The probability-scoring and CDF foundations are discussed by [Gneiting and Raftery (2007)](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf), whose relevant full-text sections were reviewed.

For random soft labels P under the actual training sampling measure, unrestricted expected cross-entropy is minimized by E[P|X]. Expected Hellinger-squared risk instead has optimum q_j proportional to (E[sqrt(P_j)|X])². The logarithmic-score/properness result in Gneiting and Raftery's section 3 supports the first statement; the second follows directly from Cauchy–Schwarz. Neither formula proves the cause of a finite network's observed failures. The CE/4 coefficient only matches local second-order scale near equality, not AdamW or clipping dynamics. A fixed H²-derived schedule and H-based checkpoint selection remain limitations of the bounded replacement experiment.

Rare screening requires support counts and task errors, not only average fit. [Davis and Goadrich (2006)](https://ftp.cs.wisc.edu/machine-learning/shavlik-group/davis.icml06.pdf) motivates precision–recall analysis under class imbalance. Here the thresholds and unique-T review budgets are fixed before evaluation; undefined precision remains undefined, and uncertain/deferred predictions on known positives remain false negatives. No AP or interpolated PR-area claim is made. Seeds repeat the same objects and are not independent biological replicates.

Raw QC quartiles are checked against integer-count support intervals compatible with the linear/type-7 convention documented in [NumPy 1.26](https://numpy.org/doc/1.26/reference/generated/numpy.quantile.html). The declared uniform-within-bin inverse-CDF proxy is a different approximation used consistently for true63 and predicted63. It is not raw type-7 reconstruction or an irreducible-error bound. Finite-bin density divides mass by actual physical width; open tails have only mass, not an invented center or width.

Native/coarsened shape support is explicitly resolution dependent. The [SiZer abstract (Chaudhuri and Marron, 1999)](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1999.10474186) motivates attending to scale; only that abstract was reviewed, and this work does not reproduce SiZer or attach biological significance to surface-vertex counts. A visible major peak permits shoulders; an unconfirmed peak is not proven absent.

The ±5 concentration bounds use original integer counts and preserve all unresolved within-bin allocations. They are identification bounds, not confidence intervals or proofs that every allocation is physically realizable. Conditional-window summaries change the measure on the same historical distribution; they do not recreate the older AF physical calculation or its source-population percentile selection.
